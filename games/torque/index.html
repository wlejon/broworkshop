Track speeds in m/s. Steering IS the difference between these two numbers — there is no steered wheel anywhere on the vehicle. Hold Space with a steer key and the tracks go equal and opposite: the hull spins on its own centre without going anywhere.

Lean

0.0° upright

Jolt's MotorcycleController adds a spring that torques the chassis toward the lean angle the corner actually needs. Switch it off and nothing else about the bike changes — same chassis, same wheels, same input — but the next corner lays it on its side. Turn it off, take a corner, turn it back on, take it again.

Tyres

—

Each preset is nothing but a pair of numbers per axle — longitudinalFriction and lateralFriction — scaling Jolt's slip curves. They multiply against the friction of the body underneath, so the same tyres behave differently again on the circuit's ice patch. Watch the slip column light up.

Every magnitude is scaled from a measured value — wheel slip off the same telemetry the table below draws, impacts off the chassis' own deceleration, the buzz off how far into the last 12% of the rev range the engine is, and the tank's low hum off track speed. Strong motor carries mass, weak motor carries texture.

The note is a synthesized waveform whose playback rate is the tachometer, crossfaded between an overrun timbre and a loaded one by the analog throttle. All three sources are attached to the chassis with attachAudioEmitter, and the listener follows the active camera — so pick Trackside, drive past the post, and watch the Doppler ratio cross 1.0. Drag this to 0 and the pass goes flat.

Camera

Chase and Bonnet are camera nodes parented to the chassis — they are re-parented onto whichever vehicle you pick and then never moved again; the scene graph carries them. Trackside is a root node aimed by hand each frame.
