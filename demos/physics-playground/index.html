Simulation

Physics steps at a fixed rate; rendering does not. Drag the step rate down to 15–20 Hz and watch a falling object: with interpolation off it teleports once per step. Turn it on (or press Space) and the same simulation glides, because the render side blends the previous and current step poses by the frame's accumulator fraction. Only what you SEE changes.

friction combine
restitution combine

Two touching bodies each carry a friction and a restitution, but a contact needs ONE of each: the combine mode decides. Drop a bouncy ball on the rubber lane (0.92) and on the ice lane (0.05), then switch between min and max: under max it bounces on both, under min it dies on the ice. default is Jolt's rule, sqrt(f1·f2) for friction and max(r1, r2) for restitution.

Pose drive

not driving

driveToPose puts position motors on every swing-twist joint and lets gravity keep working: the ragdoll develops muscle tone but still falls, sags and gets pushed around. Set it once; the motors persist until go limp. Kinematic drive instead sets part velocities so each part reaches its target transform every step: it stands the figure up and shoves whatever is in the way. Drop a ragdoll, punch it over, then drive it with the checkbox off and on.

Cloth

Pressurized ball

The gas coefficient of a closed mesh. At 300 the ball is a wet bag that slaps the lane and stays there; at 6000 it is a drum and rebounds most of the way back. There is no runtime setter, so moving the slider rebuilds and re-drops the ball, which is what you want for an A/B anyway.

SixDOF machines

slew — winch — piston —

A SixDOF constraint is six independent switches (three translations, three rotations), each locked, limited to a range, or free, and each able to carry a motor. That is not a joint, that is a machine tool.

Slew is a velocity motor: set a rate and the crane turns forever. Winch and piston are position motors: set a place and the machine goes there and holds it, loaded or not.

Axis configuration

Bars beside each pivot, X red / Y green / Z blue: a short dark stub is locked, a medium bar limited, a long glowing bar free. Rotation axes wear a collar of pips. Flip the piston's tX to free and the platform slides off its own lift.

collideConnected

centre separation —

Two 0.5 m spheres on a rope capped at 0.4 m, shorter than the sum of their radii, so the flag decides which wins. Off (the default) the pair is excluded from collision for the constraint's lifetime and the spheres merge; on, contact survives the joint and pushes them apart past the rope's own limit.

Mechanism bench

gear, rackAndPinion and pulley. Note the shape of the first two: they couple two existing constraint handles rather than building their own joints.

rack travel —

The pulley needs no motor at all: 120 kg on one end, 25 kg on the other, one rope of fixed total length over two pivots.

Breakable joints

broken / total —

Every joint in the bridge carries a breakingImpulse in N·s. When the impulse the solver needed to hold that joint closed exceeds it in one step, Jolt disables the constraint and getBrokenConstraints() reports its handle: a whole destruction system for one number per joint. The hangers go first, their load transfers to their neighbours, and the deck unzips.

Contacts & impacts

events/frame 0 impact 0 N·s

An added event carries the solver's own manifold: up to four world-space points, the contact normal, penetration depth, and an impulse estimate in kg·m/s. A negative penetration is a speculative contact (Jolt predicted a touch it has not solved yet) and the list labels those spec. The impulse scales with mass and closing speed, so it is the natural input for impact sounds, damage and particles; here it drives sparks, a flash, camera shake and the meter. Turn the drawing off and the sparks still fire; turn the effects off and the data is still drawn.
