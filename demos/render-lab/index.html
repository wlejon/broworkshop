Reflections & decals

The mirror strip at the front of the courtyard is the test surface. Tilt down until the spheres leave the frame and their reflection dies with them: that is the screen-space limit.

box 26×12×22 at y 5
The chrome sphere is near-black with the probe off: the courtyard has no IBL environment, so a metal has nothing to reflect until a probe gives it one.
SSR vs probe. SSR is accurate but screen-bound: only what is on screen reflects, and hits fade at the borders. The probe is stable and works off-screen, but it is a static box-projected cubemap. SSR wins where its ray hits; the probe is the miss fallback. Toggle each alone to see where one gives up and the other takes over.

Left-click the scene to stamp one. It raycasts to the surface and orients the projection to the hit normal: walls take them sideways, floors top-down.

LOD chains

Twelve props, one chain each: 32×24 sphere, 10×7 sphere, box, switching at 30 / 55 units. The multiplier scales both thresholds, so every switch can be forced from here instead of by flying the camera. Levels are read back from lodLevel per frame: the renderer decides, this panel only reports.

Eight obelisks, each paired with a flat imposter at the same spot: detail is gated 0 to cutoff, imposter cutoff to ∞. Unlike LOD this is a hard on/off: drag the cutoff and watch the swap walk down the avenue. Set margin to 0 and park the camera on a boundary to see the strobe the hysteresis exists to prevent.
Custom shaders

Fragment hook: value-noise threshold + discard, with an emissive band riding the front. Its shadow stays solid: the fragment chunk never reaches the shared depth-only shadow program.

Vertex hook on an 88×60 plane hung off the right back wall. Normals are rebuilt from the analytic derivative, so the ripple SHADES; the shadow pass runs the same hook, so the shadow ripples too. cullMargin = 1.6 keeps the billow from being culled outside the flat plane's AABB.

The button strips every custom program; the three props snap back to plain PBR, unchanged in every other respect. The same chunks compile for skinned and instanced meshes too (demos/anim-lab has the skinned case).
Scene as texture

That thumbnail is a second scene graph on its own canvas with its own camera, lights and post stack. sub.asTexture() into setBaseColorTexture() puts it on the screen bolted to the back wall, live. Its context is created first so it renders BEFORE the courtyard; otherwise the monitor would show last frame.
Culling & debug

Culling

Both are strictly conservative: pixels are identical either way, only the counters move. Turn frustum culling off and the drawn count jumps to the whole graph. Hold the camera still and the shadow cache reuses tiles (c climbs, r falls to 0); orbit and the directional cascades re-render. These two sit OUTSIDE the A/B toggle: they are performance mechanisms, not looks.
